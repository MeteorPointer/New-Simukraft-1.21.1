# Getting Started: Create a New City | Creation Chapter

The origin of every beginning, a ray of gentle morning light — you set off on a new adventure, and this time, you will become the Mayor.{spacing=8}

:item[simukraft:city_core]

This is the City Core. Use it to create your city. Every new city starts with a $20 starter fund.

City names must not duplicate an existing one. At least 2 characters, and no special symbols.

:::right

If the "World Population" in the upper right changes and a citizen joins your city, your city has been created successfully.

> Can't find your citizen? Search carefully. If it's truly nowhere to be found, the creation may have failed.

&nbsp;

> Once placed, the City Core cannot be moved. If you really must move it, delete and recreate the city via City Core → Edit City → Delete City — but your funds and citizens will be gone with it.

&nbsp;

> Want to manage your city anytime, anywhere? Craft a Portable City Core — right-click to open the city menu remotely~

## Choose a Building to Build

Pick a residence first! Note: constructing any building consumes money, materials, and time.

Apart from the "Studio Unit", most buildings need some construction materials. On your first day of pioneering, this is the recommended pick.

# Construction Chapter | Construction Chapter

## Building Preview

This mod adds a building preview feature, letting you decide a building's exact position without moving the build box.

Double-click the chosen building to open its details, then click "Preview" to enter preview mode.

Use WSAD to move the camera, arrow keys ↑↓←→ to move the preview, +/- keys to lower/raise its height, and Enter to start construction.

:::right

## Architect at Work...

Your first building is under construction! While waiting, go gather more materials and get ready for the next step~

Every block placed earns the architect 1 XP. As experience piles up, NPCs level up and work faster.

> Tip: press F12 to toggle RTS cursor mode — double-click a control box to open it remotely (within 128 blocks); press M to expand the minimap and survey your whole city~

# Settling In: Develop Your City | Development Chapter

## The Way of Population

If the city has vacant residences, a new citizen will join your city at midday (6000 ticks) each day.

Adult citizens also fall in love and marry: singles have a chance each day to meet their sweetheart. Once wed, the couple moves into the same residence — a sweet little life for two~

If their household has 3 or more beds, the married couple may also have children — kids grow a year older each day, and at 18 they become adults who can take up jobs and help build the city~

:::right

## City Levels

Your city starts at Level 1 "Pioneer" and climbs all the way to Level 12 "City That Never Sleeps"!

Level-ups require population, funds, and materials (logs and cobblestone at first, later iron ingots, gold ingots, even diamonds). Submit the application at the city core menu and wait for approval.

Higher levels allow more claimed chunks and more enclaves — Level 12 "City That Never Sleeps" has no chunk limit and allows up to 15 enclaves!

# Settling In: Develop Your City | Development Chapter

## Claiming Land

City territory doesn't come free — you have to buy it.

Open the city core menu's map page: right-click a chunk and pick "Claim Chunk" to buy a single plot; hold the middle mouse button (scroll wheel) and drag to box-select a whole area for bulk claiming (up to 256 chunks).

Chunks connected to your territory cost $10 each; disconnected enclaves cost $50 each (unlocked at level 4).

:::right

Unwanted chunks can also be released via right-click → "Abandon Chunk".

# Settling In: Develop Your City | Development Chapter

## The Way of Rent

Every morning, citizens living in a home pay rent. The rent is tied to the building's construction price — the pricier the home, the higher the rent!

Therefore, only building cheap homes like the "Studio Unit" spares you the chore of gathering materials, but yields very little rent.

Stock up materials now and build something pricier (say, $5–7)!

:::right

Out of land? Raise your city level first, then claim new land on the map page — the chunk cap rises with your level~

# Settling In: Develop Your City | Development Chapter

## Feed Your Citizens

After developing your city this way, you may notice a problem: your citizens are starving!

That's right — citizens have hunger too. When hungry, their work efficiency drops, and they may even die.

:::right

The simplest way is to feed citizens by hand. From enchanted golden apples all the way down to rotten flesh (if you want your citizens to get food poisoning...), you can toss any food at them to restore their hunger.

Of course, tossing food by hand gets exhausting once citizens multiply. As mayor, we have a better way to solve this.

This is where — commercial buildings come into play.

# Settling In: Develop Your City | Development Chapter

## Build a Commercial Building

Commercial buildings are diverse — some sell food to citizens, some sell resources to players, and some buy items from players and pay them currency.

To feed citizens, we can build shops that sell food — for example, the butcher shop, the fruit shop, and the bakery.

:::right

For example, we start with the "Butcher Shop", a simple building that sells cooked meat to citizens. Provide it with raw beef or raw porkchops, hire a butcher, and he will cook the meat and sell it to hungry citizens!

> Want to trade with a shop? Right-click the shopkeeper themselves to open the trade screen. Shopkeepers work their business hours — if stock is low, just wait for restocking~

# Settling In: Develop Your City | Development Chapter

## When Citizens Fall Ill

Citizens are made of flesh and blood — low health or a bout of flu will make them set down their work and go to the hospital for treatment.

Build a hospital in your city (with beds and a Medical Control Box) and hire a doctor to open it. Patients recover 1 HP every 100 ticks; a full day of hospitalization is needed to fully recover.

:::right

A hospital covers a certain chunk area around its control box. Only families within coverage can have babies smoothly.

> Without a hospital or a free bed, a sick citizen can only lie at home — and won't get better. If you want them back on their feet soon, don't skip the hospital~

> If the doctor is sick or on leave, the hospital pauses admissions. Build a second hospital — one more safety net!

# Taking Root: Toward Retirement Life! | Taking Root Chapter

## Lie Back and Get Materials

Tired of gathering building materials and food by hand? You need industrial buildings!

After constructing an industrial building, right-click its control box to open the interface.

Once you hire a worker, production begins according to the chosen recipe. Raw materials are drawn from the building's containers, and products go back into them. Generally speaking, higher worker levels yield more output (+5% per level).

:::right

![](industry.png){width=256 height=148}

# Taking Root: Toward Retirement Life! | Taking Root Chapter

Another great place to obtain building materials — the Building Material Merchant.

The Building Material Merchant is a commercial building. Once a shopkeeper is hired, it sells various building materials to players.

Note, however, that commercial buildings have stock limits — you can't buy infinitely.

When stock runs out, shopkeepers restock automatically on their own schedule. You can also build multiple shops to expand stock.

:::right

Meanwhile, the Lumberjack's Hut is an industrial building: the lumberjack fells natural trees within 32 blocks of its work area and consumes saplings to replant — keep the lumberjack's inventory stocked with saplings, and the logs will pile up in the output container.

# Taking Root: Toward Retirement Life! | Taking Root Chapter

## Logistics System

To save the labor of moving items between commercial and industrial buildings, you can set up a logistics system.

Start by building a warehouse! Design one stuffed with containers and place a "Logistics Server" inside.

:item[simukraft:logistics_server_box]

:::right

![](save.png){width=256 height=128}

If you want a quick start, choose the Small Town Warehouse from the "Public" category in the Build Box and let citizens build it for you~

# Taking Root: Toward Retirement Life! | Taking Root Chapter

Right-click the Logistics Server with an empty hand, hire a warehouse manager, and bind containers.

Then place Logistics Clients where items need to be transferred, and bind containers — almost every commercial and industrial building comes with one built in.

Create routes on the server, and the manager schedules transfers automatically. If the manager takes leave, logistics pauses too.

:::right

Logistics is great, but transfers aren't free.

Each transfer costs $0.02; for routes longer than 256 blocks, each additional 64 blocks adds $0.01. Keep your factories close to the warehouse and your funds will thank you!

# Taking Root: Toward Retirement Life! | Taking Root Chapter

## Mining? Leave It to the Drill!

Late-game material consumption is huge? Build a drilling rig and automate mining too!

Right-click the ground with the Geological Hammer (durability 800, 1 point per survey) to scan for virtual ore veins within 256 blocks above and below. The ores sleep quietly underground, waiting for the drill to wake them~

Place a Drilling Control Box on the rig and hire a driller to start. Use the shallow bit at Y≥10; the deep bit for anything deeper. Drilling downward consumes drill rods — the deeper you go, the more it costs.

:::right

Mined ores flow automatically into the rig's barrels. Different ores produce on their own cycles — a rich vein can yield several batches a day!

Different veins lie at different depths — survey with the Geological Hammer before deciding where to build the rig.

> Don't lose heart when the hammer says "no vein" — about three in ten fields are born empty. Just move 256+ blocks away and try again~

## Common Vein Distribution

By depth: shallow ground (Y 0–48) holds bituminous coal; mid depths (Y 16–80) hold hematite; near the surface (Y 48–96) you'll find lignite, limonite, and malachite. Bornite, pyrite, and lapis hide at Y -32–16; gold sits at Y -56–-16; diamond and redstone only appear at the very bottom, Y -64–-32; and emerald does the opposite, lurking only in the mountains at Y 96–192~

:::right

Veins are also picky about terrain — just remember these rhymes: "Gold grips the roots, go deep below" — probe to the very bottom at the foot of great snowy mountains; "Emeralds crown the peaks" — prospect on the shoulders of snowy summits; "Redstone? Any ridge will do" — punch through the inland hills anywhere; it's the best fallback deep ore.

Iron depends on the sky: dig hematite in hot, dry badlands and savannas; limonite beside humid swamps and jungles; pyrite hides only deep in high mountains. Copper depends on the ground: malachite on flat desert land; bornite and chalcopyrite in warm mountains. Coal depends on water: bituminous coal in the foothills; lignite by the water~

# Sprouting: Realize Your Metropolis Dream! | Sprouting Chapter

## Bank and Exchange

Once your city has wealth, it's time to make money with money!

Build a bank and hire a teller: officials can deposit and withdraw coins — deposits flow into city funds, withdrawals return to your pocket. You can even transfer funds to other cities.

:::right

The Exchange is your city's stock market: it opens at 8:00 and closes at 20:00, repricing every hour. Hire a broker, and officials can buy and sell stocks with city funds; the portfolio belongs to the city.

Stock prices drift back toward a "fair value" computed from how well the city runs — the more prosperous the city, the stronger the price!

# Sprouting: Realize Your Metropolis Dream! | Sprouting Chapter

## Global Market

Want to trade with other cities? Build a Global Market and hire a market trader!

Officials list offers at their own prices: for sell offers, goods go into the market warehouse and proceeds flow to the city; for buy offers, the city pays and goods go into storage.

:::right

Right-click the market trader to browse offers from every city in the dimension — cross-city shopping and exports made easy.

> Prices are set by the listing city. Shop around for the best deal~

As the city grows, old buildings (like "Studio Unit") may look out of place. Hire a planner to demolish them, then manually remove the building's control box. Residents will automatically move to new buildings.
